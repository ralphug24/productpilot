How to run the session

Study question Can three first time players get into the same WordRush room and understand their first few rounds with little or no help from you?

Player 1 hosts

They share their screen first, create a room, and invite the other two players.

Players 2 and 3 join

They use their own devices and the invitation from Player 1. Everyone plays as themselves.

You observe

Use the call to watch and listen. Keep the note sheet on your own screen and stay out of the game.

Before the call

  1. Recruit three adults who have not played WordRush. Explain the study, permissions, and 25 to 30 minute time commitment. Keep names and contact details outside this sheet.
  2. Ask each person to have the public game link ready. Agree which person will host; give everyone anonymous codes such as P01, P02, and P03.
  3. Use a video call with screen sharing. The host shares first. Keep the game build stable across sessions and check its rules before the study.
  4. Use the form below to record the group code, date, devices, and each participant's permission. Recording requires separate agreement; this kit does not record the call.

During the call

  1. Introductions and permission, about 5 minutes. Read the opening script and ask for background information.
  2. Create and join, about 5 minutes. Give the shared task. The host sends the invitation and the other two join. Observe what they do before helping.
  3. First rounds, about 5 to 10 minutes. Ask them to begin when ready. Observe at least three rounds, including feedback and coordination.
  4. Individual debrief, about 5 to 10 minutes. Ask each person for their own reaction and ease rating, ideally privately in the call chat before they hear the others' ratings.

If they get stuck After about five minutes on setup, offer help if necessary. Record the help and select the outcome that says help was needed. A time limit here is a practical session guide, not a performance target for the product.

One shared screen shows one viewpoint. You can see the host's actions and the lobby roster, but you may not see how joiners navigated their screens. Ask a joiner to share briefly if useful, or label their account as The player told me this. Seeing a player arrive in the host's lobby confirms arrival; it does not establish how they joined.

How to report the study Three players together are one group session with three participants. Group discussion and advice between players affect the experience. Record that assistance and avoid presenting the group as three independent usability sessions. For more evidence, repeat with another group. Optional host rotation is a learned second attempt and should be labelled separately.

Recruitment message you can copy

I am researching WordRush, a browser word game I built. Would you join two other people for a 25 to 30 minute online session? You will play together while I observe and take notes. I want to understand what is clear and where people get stuck. No gaming experience is required, and participation is optional.

Keep observations separate from interpretations and exact quotes. This note taking approach follows the GOV.UK research analysis guidance. The group format and its reporting limits above are the design of this exploratory WordRush study.

What to say

Opening

Thanks for helping. You will play WordRush together while I watch and take notes. Please use it as you normally would and tell me when something surprises you. You can skip a question or stop at any time. I built the game, and critical feedback is useful.

May I take written notes? Separately, may I use deidentified notes and quotes in ProductPilot, including analysis by Cloudflare Workers AI, to identify improvements? An anonymized quote in the public case study would need your separate agreement. Recording would also need separate permission.

Record these choices for each person. If someone declines written notes, stop collecting their data and arrange a suitable session. AI permission is optional; agreed notes can remain for manual review. Group notes require all three participants' AI permission before this kit will export them for AI analysis.

Background

Ask each person: Have you used WordRush before? What device and browser are you using? How often do you play games with friends online?

1 Get everyone into one room

Imagine you want to play WordRush together. Get all three of you into the same room.

Direct this to the agreed host without naming controls. Let participants coordinate. Record whether they help each other as well as any help you provide. Check the host's lobby roster for arrivals.

2 Play the first rounds

Start when you think everyone is ready. Play the first few rounds as you normally would.

Watch at least three rounds if possible. Let the group play without continuous questions during time sensitive actions. Ask about surprises between rounds. If a joiner's screen is not visible, keep their later explanation labelled as a report.

3 Ask each person separately

  1. How easy was it to get into the game, from 1 very difficult to 7 very easy? What made you choose that number?
  2. How do you think a player earns a point? Compare the explanation with the rules you checked for the mode used.
  3. What was the most confusing moment for you?
  4. What would you change first, and why?
  5. Would you invite someone to play again? What would affect that choice?

If a participant asks for help

First ask “What would you expect to happen?” Record any instruction you eventually give. Also record instructions from another participant. Mark the setup outcome as assisted when navigation help was needed.

Close the session

Thank everyone, check any quote publication permission, and save a backup file. Do not make a product decision while the evidence is still being collected.

Questions to investigate

  • Do players understand when to create a room and when to join one?
  • Can a host get a second player into the intended room without navigation help?
  • Do players recognize when the lobby is ready and how to begin?
  • Can players explain the rules after the first round?

These questions do not establish a competitive weakness. Prioritize an improvement only after participant evidence identifies a meaningful problem. Recheck competitor behavior and sources before publishing the final matrix.

Use the evidence in ProductPilot

Choose WordRush — First-game experience in ProductPilot. That project is saved in its current visitor profile and browser. You can create the same project in another browser and upload your exported evidence there.

  1. Run the group session. Use the kit yourself while the three participants use WordRush.
  2. Review participant codes, permissions, observations, and quotes. Check which reports came from an unseen screen.
  3. Click Save backup file. Keep that file in a location you control. To continue on another device, open this page there, click Load backup file, and select the file. The file extension .json is just the backup format; you do not have to read or edit it.
  4. Export quotes and observations as separate TXT files. Use Upload files in the WordRush project and choose AI research synthesis.
  5. Review the proposed opportunities and their exact source evidence. Check the counts manually: one group of three is one group session. Several notes from a person are not several users.
  6. Prioritize one improvement, record your rationale, and draft a reviewed PRD. Establish a baseline before proposing a target, then evaluate a prototype in a later group session.

Data flow You observe WordRush → write notes here → save or export files → upload evidence to ProductPilot. The kit has no automatic WordRush data feed. ProductPilot sends selected excerpts to Cloudflare Workers AI only when you choose an AI operation.

Keep planning material separate Do not upload this script, blank form, competitor matrix, or unverified hypotheses as participant research. The final case study should disclose your role as the game's creator, the group format, visible screens, help given, participant count, sample limits, and the permission for any published quote.

An optional later host rotation can reveal a new role perspective, but participants have already learned the game. Keep that evidence labelled as a second attempt rather than another first time user.
