Focus-group prompts

  1. What was the experience like from beginning to end?
  2. Where did you feel unsure, stuck, or need help?
  3. What made the game easy or enjoyable?

Preparation and analysis reference
Recording → transcript → ProductPilot
  1. Download the Zoom recording and transcript after the session. Check the transcript against the recording, especially unknown or overlapping speakers.
  2. Replace names with the participant codes used here. Label your own turns Moderator:. Keep timestamps. A useful text format is [05:20] P01: …, with each speaker's turn on its own line.
  3. Use the event timestamps to revisit important moments. Add detail to bookmarks only when the recording or your notes support it. A bookmark by itself is a reminder, not a finding.
  4. With the agreed permissions, upload the deidentified transcript as TXT, DOCX, or a selectable-text PDF to the WordRush project in ProductPilot. Zoom cloud transcripts are VTT files; convert or save the reviewed text as TXT first. Raw VTT upload is not supported by ProductPilot.
  5. Choose AI research synthesis, check the evidence-linked opportunities, prioritize an improvement, and review its PRD. You can also code the transcript in NVivo/MAXQDA and analyze form responses in R/Python as usual.

Zoom can leave speakers unidentified. Review attribution rather than assuming every label is correct. Zoom transcript guidance ↗

Consent and study-form details

The connected consent/pre-study form uses Clemson's adult consent template as a structural guide, adapted for this WordRush study. It explains purpose, activities, a session under 20 minutes, voluntary participation, risks/benefits, group-discussion privacy, and the researcher's contact: ralphugboko@gmail.com. It does not claim institutional approval.

Participation, recording, AI processing, measurement use, and public quotations have distinct Yes/No choices. Declining participation or recording ends the intake before background questions. An optional-use refusal does not prevent participation. Review all three responses before recording or using a group transcript for AI analysis.

Pre-study form

  • Consent, participant and session codes, previous WordRush use.
  • Optional device/browser and familiarity with multiplayer browser games.

Post-study form

  • Matching codes and full-task ease from 1 very difficult to 7 very easy.
  • Help needed, confusing moments, what worked, desired improvements, and interest in playing again.

These are study-specific questions, not a validated usability questionnaire. Forms collect individual ratings; the recorded group discussion captures the shared conversation. Use ProductPilot and compare sessions later

ProductLens is your case study. Use the transcript, observer notes, and post-study responses as evidence. ProductPilot supports synthesis, opportunities, prioritization, and PRDs.

For a before/after comparison, keep the same task endpoint, instructions, help policy, and game mode. Record versions and prior exposure. The key outcome here is completing that task without researcher guidance. Starting a round is one milestone within the journey.

Report one group session with three participants. People influence one another during play and the focus-group discussion. Peer help is part of the group experience; distinguish it from researcher intervention. Do not treat the group as three independent usability sessions.

Decision Lab currently analyzes simulated experiments, including fixed-group designs and completion/timing outcomes. It cannot yet import real study measurements or survey ratings. Use NVivo/MAXQDA and R/Python for your current analysis. A small moderated comparison is descriptive evidence, not proof of causal uplift.

Questions to investigate

  • Do players understand when to create a room and when to join one?
  • Can a host get a second player into the intended room without navigation help?
  • Do players recognize when the lobby is ready and how to begin?
  • Can players explain the rules after the first round?

These questions do not establish a competitive weakness. Prioritize an improvement only after participant evidence identifies a meaningful problem. Recheck competitor behavior and sources before publishing the final matrix.
