Try a complete product review.

Help a product team decide what to improve in onboarding, using interview evidence to create a reviewed product requirements document (PRD).

10–15 minutes4 tasksFeedback: 3–5 minutes
← Back to workspace

Before you start

Select the Onboarding experience project and use the interviews already in its research library. Keep this guide open alongside the workspace. Try each task on your own first; open a hint if you get stuck. There is no time limit and no right priority to choose.

If you have an existing review, select Start new review for this session. Your documents, opportunity backlog, and previous decisions are retained. Note your start and finish times, any hints you use, and anything that surprises you.

01

Find the customer problem

Find and read an interview describing an onboarding difficulty. Identify one problem in the participant’s own words.

You’re done when: you can name the interview and point to a passage that describes the problem.

Need a hint?

Open Research library or Customer feedback. Use the document name or Read transcript to open an interview. Search can match words inside transcripts. Click outside the dialog, press Esc, or use × to close it.

02

Check an opportunity against its evidence

Generate opportunities from the interviews. Choose one proposal and check whether its source quotes support the problem statement.

You’re done when: you can trace a quote to the full interview and say whether the proposal matches the evidence.

Need a hint?

In Product workflow → Customer feedback, choose transcripts and select Analyze selected transcripts. On an opportunity, expand Read source quotes, then select Open full transcript. Use Edit opportunity wording if the proposal needs a correction.

03

Make a product decision

Compare at least two opportunities. Choose what the team should address first and explain your reasoning, including one trade-off.

You’re done when: your selected opportunity and rationale are saved, and a PRD draft appears.

Need a hint?

Select opportunities to compare, then open Prioritization. RICE estimates are optional: Reach is users per quarter, Impact is 1–3, Confidence is 1–100%, and Effort is person-weeks. Select Select this opportunity to draft requirements, write why it matters now, choose a drafting method, then select Save decision & draft requirements. A PRD is a Product Requirements Document. Unselected opportunities remain in the backlog.

04

Review and share the product brief

Inspect the draft, make one useful edit, and record an unresolved question. Mark the PRD reviewed, download the Word document, and open it to assess readability.

You’re done when: you can find the product decision, requirements, and source evidence in the downloaded document.

Need a hint?

In Product brief, edit the draft fields and check proposed requirements against the evidence. Confirm the review checkbox and select Mark PRD reviewed. Use Download Word document (.docx) and open it in Word or another DOCX reader. Saved decisions also appear in Decision log.

FINISH WITH YOUR FEEDBACK

Tell us how it went.

For each task, report whether you finished independently, used a hint or help, could not finish, or did not try. Tell us what felt confusing, how readable the PRD was, any errors, and the one change you would make first. Contact details are optional.
